# Role-model references (v2)

Rule: every element borrows a proven principle from a named game or product. We never copy assets, characters, music or recognisable designs. Items marked *unverified* should be checked against footage.

## 1. Core frame

| Afferent element | Role model and concrete mechanic | Why it works there | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The director | **Left 4 Dead, AI Director** (Valve, Michael Booth, GDC 2009): estimates each player's intensity and cycles build-up, peak and relax phases by changing spawns, items and music | Pacing follows the player, so no two runs feel the same and tension never stays maxed | Our "intensity" is the body: pulse against the personal baseline and eye closure. Instead of building tension we move the player towards a calmer or more awake state |
| Adaptivity without punishment | **Flow** (thatgamecompany, Jenova Chen, *Flow in Games*, 2006): difficulty adapts so the player stays between boredom and anxiety | Players stay engaged without a difficulty menu | Task intensity follows the mode and drops after mistakes; nothing can be failed |
| Anti-model | **Nevermind** (Flying Mollusk): the game gets harder as measured stress rises | Shows that biofeedback which punishes arousal builds an anxiety loop | High pulse makes our game slower and kinder, never harder; the lantern never mirrors a racing heart |
| One-finger play | **Alto's Odyssey** (one-touch control; Zen Mode with no score and no reset) | Instantly learnable, playable lying down | Every task uses one finger; the figure walks by itself |
| Colour per task, dawn | **Gris** (colour returns to the world chapter by chapter); **Journey** (summit ending, no score) | The reward is atmosphere, not points | Each finished task returns one colour; the session always ends at full-colour dawn |
| Fatigue measure | **Driver drowsiness systems** (PERCLOS, share of time the eyes are closed) | Robust, simple, widely deployed in cars | Eye closure over the last 20 s; it is the only thing the camera reads |
| Morning light | **Sunrise alarm clocks** (for example wake-up lights) | Light rising before or at waking is a familiar, gentle wake-up | In morning sessions the bedside light rises to daylight at dawn and in fatigue mode; at night it stays dim |

## 2. Tasks

| Task | Role model | Why it works there | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breath | **Apple Watch Breathe** (expanding and contracting shape, haptic taps); **Celeste** chapter 4 (breathing keeps a feather afloat) | Pacing is learnt in seconds | Circle grows 4 s, shrinks 6 s; hold while it grows; haptics at each inhale; fixed at 6 per minute |
| Löyly | **Among Us: Swipe Card** (swipe at the right speed or it fails) | A familiar, slightly tricky motion | Here the right speed is slow: a pour under 2 s only splashes, a slow one fills the sauna with steam. Calming by design |
| Aurora threads | **Among Us: Fix Wiring** (drag coloured wires to matching ends) | Clear goal, satisfying connection | Threads of northern light; a wrong match snaps back |
| Constellation | **Among Us: Unlock Manifolds** (tap numbers in order) | Simple ordering, light focus | Tap stars from smallest to largest; no numbers needed |
| Fireflies | **Among Us: Clear Asteroids** (tap moving targets) | Fast, lively, rewarding | Count and speed rise with intensity; the jar glows brighter |
| Beacon | **Among Us: Calibrate Distributor** (tap when the rotating part aligns) | Timing task with a clear moment | The lighthouse beam must touch the boat; speed rises with intensity |
| Bells | **Among Us: Start Reactor** (repeat a growing sequence) | Short working-memory task, activating | Three bells; after two mistakes the sequence gets shorter |
| Shutters | **Among Us: Swipe Card** (swipe gesture) | One clear gesture, done in seconds | Swiping each shutter up lets morning light into the panel and the world |
| Task panel and progress dots | **Among Us** task panels (modal card, close and come back, progress bar) | Everyone who has played a party game knows the pattern | Wordless dark-glass card, small glowing dots for progress, thin time bar |

## 3. World, sound and UI

| Element | Role model | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Goal on the horizon | **Journey** (the mountain is always visible) | The lighthouse stands on the horizon all session |
| Sound grows with the world | **Proteus** (music emerges from the world; *unverified detail*) | Each mode has its own tempo and density; no fail sounds |
| Start screen with "Not tonight" | **Alto's Odyssey Zen Mode** (the player picks low stakes) | Two equal buttons; declining is neutral data |
| Crisis card | **SPARX** (Merry et al., BMJ 2012; excluded adolescents at major self-harm risk) | Crisis is a referral matter outside gameplay: fixed card, never AI |
