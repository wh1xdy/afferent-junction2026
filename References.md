# Role-model references

Rule: every station, mechanic, transition, sound and UI element borrows a proven principle from a named game or app. We never copy assets, characters, music or recognisable designs. Details marked *unverified* were not confirmed by the references research and should be checked against gameplay footage.

## 1. Core frame

| Afferent element | Role model and concrete moment | Why it works there | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Side view, auto-walk | Alto's Odyssey (the character moves forward by itself, one input); Ring Fit Adventure Knee Assist (jogging automated) | Removes steering, so all attention goes to one meaningful action | The figure walks itself. Only the body acts: eyes, gaze, breath |
| Goal visible on the horizon | Journey (the mountain is visible from the first dune) | Direction without a map or words | The lighthouse sits at the right edge from second 3 and only grows |
| Colour returns per station | Gris (each chapter returns one colour to the world) | The reward is atmosphere, not points. Progress without a score | Yellow, blue, green, then dawn. Purely visual, so the music carries the payoff too (Gris also ties colours to abilities; we do not) |
| No fail state | Gris (no death); Alto's Odyssey Zen Mode ("no scores, no coins, no power-ups", a fall does not reset the run) | Players relax and explore because nothing can be lost | A miss only delays the bloom (5 s instead of 1.5 s), never a smaller reward. Note: the research suggested partial colour on a miss. That was overruled by the patient advocate and art director, see `Decisions.md` |
| Vitals-reactive world | DEEP (slow breaths move you through the world); Flower (body movement steers something soft); **anti-model: Nevermind** (the game gets harder as measured fear rises) | DEEP and Flower make the body feel like a gentle controller. Nevermind shows that biofeedback that punishes arousal builds an anxiety loop, and its developer found webcam emotion input weaker than a heart-rate sensor | Pulse and eyes change only style: cooler and slower world when pulse is high, sparkle when it rises in activating segments. Never harder, never blocking. Emotion recognition is dropped |

## 2. Stations

| Station / mechanic | Role model and concrete moment | Why it works there | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calibration cued by the lantern | Before Your Eyes (per-person blink calibration at the start); Journey (teaches with no text) | A short personal calibration makes later blink input feel fair. Journey proves cues can be wordless | Lantern steady (open), dims (close), flares (wide), rests. Each phase has a tiny eye glyph plus a sound cue because Before Your Eyes needs text and we do not. No wink check |
| Lantern check-in | Apple Health "State of Mind" (iOS 17, a single shape slides from unpleasant to pleasant; details *unverified*) | A one-item mood log people already know, low burden | 5 flame sizes, same warm hue, dimmest still beautiful. Set by head tilt or tap, confirmed by long blink. Colour choice was dropped (colour implies a right answer and is not ordinal) |
| Forest: lift your gaze | Flower (a gentle tilt steers petals); Sky: Children of the Light (carried candlelight pushes back darkness; *partly unverified*) | Continuous, soft, analogue response with no target line | Head pitch maps continuously to lantern radius. Framed as looking up to the light, never as posture |
| Fork: effort choice | Celeste strawberries (an optional harder route for a reward with no mechanical value) | Players choose effort freely. Not choosing is fine | Summit costs 3 s of eyes wide for a bonus (shooting stars). Flat path stays beautiful. No comment on the choice |
| Fork: how you choose | Before Your Eyes ("look, then blink" to activate; mouse click accepted as a blink) | Selection by gaze plus confirmation is robust and accessible | Head turn highlights a path, long blink or tap confirms. Wink was dropped as a selector (many people cannot wink) |
| Lake: blink at the flash | Animal Crossing fishing (random wait, a clear splash, react; a miss means you cast again; details *unverified*) | A reaction task with a natural random foreperiod and a gentle miss | Jittered 1.5 to 3.5 s foreperiod, soft warm flash plus bell, blink to send light through the ice. A miss still glows, later. Optional later: soft pre-glints like fake nibbles to catch anticipations |
| Sauna: paced breathing | Apple Watch Breathe (rings expand and contract, haptic taps); Celeste chapter 4 gondola (breathing keeps a feather afloat); DEEP (breath moves you) | Pacing is learned in seconds. Breathing as a mechanic is known and calm | 4 s in, 6 s out. Because a visual pacer is invisible with closed eyes, pacing moves to haptics and a swelling pad. Lesson from DEEP (its belt slipping frustrated players): never gate progress on pulse |
| Sauna: lights on opening the eyes | Before Your Eyes (the world changes while your eyes are shut) | Closing the eyes becomes a moment of anticipation, opening becomes the reveal | The aurora and the pad resolution arrive on the same frame the eyes open |
| Lighthouse: eyes wide to bring dawn | Journey final ascent (emotional template); Before Your Eyes (keep-your-eyes-open moments) | Sustained effort right before the release makes the payoff land | 2 s cumulative eyes wide, threshold halves after 3 s (strain guard), then the Gris-style colour sweep |
| Dawn colour sweep | Gris (colour washes into the world at a chapter end) | One big visual release | Left to right sweep through all layers in 4 s, synced with the bedside LED |
| Keep the moment | Before Your Eyes (a blink takes a photo); Alba: A Wildlife Adventure (photographing moments) | Capturing a moment is familiar and gentle | One long blink or tap on the end screen keeps tonight's dawn as a glowing stone. Replaces double blink (false triggers). Never shown as a collection |

## 3. World, sound and UI

| Element | Role model and concrete moment | Why it works there | Afferent adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glowing screen border | iPhone front-camera Retina Flash (technical proof that screen light lights a face); Sky: Children of the Light candle (diegetic light) | Screen light is a real light source; diegetic light never feels like UI | The border is the lantern's light falling on the player, 10% at rest, max 50% only in the sauna |
| One sound voice per colour | Proteus (objects carry their own sound and the music emerges from them; *unverified detail*); Journey (score swells at the summit) | Music that grows with the world feels earned without a score | Pentatonic piano, one added voice per colour (cello, glass bell, pad, full phrase). No fail sounds |
| Start screen with "Not tonight" | Alto's Odyssey Zen Mode (the player chooses low stakes). No strong game model for declining | Choosing the low-stakes mode is respected | Two equal buttons. Declining is neutral data, never failure |
| End screen without score | Journey (ends on the summit, no score) | The feeling is the result | Dawn, the figure rests, "Rest well." |
| Skip and auto-complete | Ring Fit Adventure Knee Assist (auto-completes jogging); Celeste Assist Mode (lowers difficulty without shame; exact options *unverified*) | Assists without shame keep people playing | Hold 1 s to skip, station timeouts, touch fallback. All flagged in the data |
| Crisis card | SPARX (Merry et al., BMJ 2012: n = 187, non-inferior to treatment as usual; excluded adolescents at major self-harm risk). In-game help routing *unverified* | Even a clinically tested game treats crisis as a referral matter outside gameplay | Fixed, human-written card with clinic, 112 and MIELI Crisis Helpline. Never AI, never triggered by game signals |
